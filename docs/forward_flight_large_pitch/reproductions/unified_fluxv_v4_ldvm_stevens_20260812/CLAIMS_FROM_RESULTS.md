## Supported claim

On these reconstructed development cases, the composite v4b diagnostic—old
FluxV UVLM retained, a phase-resolved paired-LDVM discrepancy, and a causal
persistence-weighted polar branch—reduces aggregate error relative to frozen
old FluxV for Yang mean lift/drag, Scherer Figure-14 mean thrust, and both
Stevens Figure-21 lift traces. It preserves the inherited v2 no-onset
Figure-11 numerical comparator.

## Unsupported stronger claim

The evidence does not establish universal generalization, superiority to the
Yang v1 or authors' models, a conservative three-dimensional LDVM–UVLM
coupling, numerical convergence, statistical significance, or an independent
held-out validation. Yang lift degrades pointwise at four of six angles even
though aggregate MAE improves; Figure 14 improves 10 of 14 observations and
has no experimental phase history; Stevens publishes no drag and was
reclassified as a development-transfer set.

## Required next confirmation

Freeze the present architecture and thresholds, then run an untouched finite-
wing dataset with phase-resolved experimental lift and drag. Before a production
claim, also complete component ablations, independent grid/time/core/wake
refinement, and a shared 3-D LEV/TE-wake Kelvin ledger.
